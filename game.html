Game proposal · OakBots United · for next year's NRL

One field. Thirty-two boxes. Every one is a decision.

Boxed In is a two-alliance robotics game where robots race through a shared diamond of 32 mystery boxes, crack them open, and turn what is inside into points. No two boxes hold the same thing, so no two matches play out the same way.

01 · Why Boxed In

Why it belongs in next year's NRL

A game that is easy to explain, hard to master, and fair to every team.

01

It tests real engineering

Robots must grab 3D-printed boxes, open latches, place cubes precisely, and fill cylinders with spheres. Each job is a design problem teams can document in their engineering portfolios.

02

Every match is different — and fair

Box contents are random, but both alliances start with the same pool of 48 cubes and 16 spheres. Luck makes matches exciting; skill decides who wins.

03

It rewards strategy, not only speed

Open boxes for points, fill cylinders for bonus boxes, or secure boxes for the endgame. The best teams read the field and adapt.

04

It makes alliances matter

Every box is free for anyone to take. Alliances have to split up the work, protect their routes, and fight for the center of the diamond.

05

It works at every skill level

A simple robot can still help by moving boxes, while advanced teams open them, build G.R.I.D. patterns, and race to fill cylinders.

06

Simple to run, easy to judge

Endgame scores are flat, the field is foam tiles with a 1-foot wall, and every scoring action is visible at a glance.

03 · The field

Two identical halves

Red and blue. Foam tiles, surrounded by a wall 1 foot high. Thirty-two boxes sit in a diamond in the middle, and every box is free for either alliance to take. Keep scrolling to tour each zone.

Zone 1 / 6

Start squares

Two per side, marked S. Every robot begins the match here.

Zone 2 / 6

Drop zone

A corner area where boxes are brought to be opened. In the endgame, unopened boxes left here still score.

Zone 3 / 6

The G.R.I.D.

A tall board — 5 columns wide and 17 cubes tall — with an outlined pattern on the back. It works like Connect 4: cubes are dropped in from the top and fall to the bottom of their column, so teams have to stack them in the right order to fill the pattern. The pattern changes randomly every match.

Zone 4 / 6

Charging cylinders

Two per side. Each holds 5 spheres. Fill one and a bonus box appears for your alliance.

Zone 5 / 6

Private delivery platforms

Two raised platforms per side, about 4 cubes tall. Bonus boxes appear here — and at the end of the match, every box sitting on a platform scores 6 points.

Zone 6 / 6

The diamond

32 mystery boxes in a diamond across the center line. Nobody owns them. Whoever gets there first decides what happens next.

04 · Game pieces

The mystery box

Each box is 3D printed and has a latch that a robot must open. Inside: anywhere from 1 to 7 items.

Cube → G.R.I.D.Sphere → cylinder
Fair by design

Random, but never unfair

Before every match, each alliance's pool of 48 cubes and 16 spheres is shuffled between its 16 starting boxes. The number of items per box is random — but both alliances always begin with the same totals. Try it:

05 · Match flow · 1 / 5

Line up

Robots start on their start squares.

Match flow · 2 / 5

Grab boxes

Robots collect boxes from the diamond and bring them back to their drop zone.

Match flow · 3 / 5

Crack them open

Robots open the boxes and sort the items. Cubes are dropped into the top of the G.R.I.D. and fall into place, Connect-4 style; spheres go into the charging cylinders.

Match flow · 4 / 5

Charge up

Fill a cylinder with 5 spheres and the team receives a bonus box with 7 items — 6 cubes and 1 sphere — on its private delivery platform.

Match flow · 5 / 5

Endgame

In the last 30 seconds the charging cylinders lock, and the endgame rules apply.

07 · The endgame

Open it
or secure it?

0:30

In the last 30 seconds, the charging cylinders lock and teams make the call that decides close matches.

Option A

Secure it

Leave a box unopened in the drop zone, or place it in private delivery. Guaranteed points, no risk.

3 drop zone · 6 delivery
Option B

Open it

Keep opening boxes and placing cubes on the G.R.I.D. for more points — but risk running out of time.

~10 avg per opened box

Because the endgame values are flat, teams always know what a decision is worth, and judges can score it instantly.

08 · In short

One simple question

Boxed In turns one simple question — open it or secure it — into a match full of decisions. It is fair by design, rich in engineering, and easy for anyone to watch and understand. It is a game built to make next year's NRL memorable.
